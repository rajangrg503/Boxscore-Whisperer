How it works

Built from his real games

Per-minute production from his own game log, multiplied by the minutes he is likely to play. Not a season average pretending tonight is an average night.

A range, not a number

Every projection comes with the band the result usually lands in. In three seasons of backtests, the real result landed inside the 80% range about 80% of the time.

Every adjustment is shown

Opponent defence, minutes, teammates out. Each layer names what it changed, by how much, and how much data stands behind it.

It says what it can't do

Describe the game in your own words and it applies only the parts it can actually measure — and lists the rest, unapplied, with the reason.

What it deliberately does not do

  • No picks. No ranked slate, no units, no "locks". A projection and its range, and you decide what that is worth.
  • No invented numbers. If there is no measured layer behind something — a double team, a change of role — it is named and left out rather than guessed at.
  • No odds and no bookmakers. Nothing here is affiliated with, or paid by, anyone taking bets.

The track record

There isn't one yet. The 2026–27 season has not started.

From opening night, a projection goes out before tip-off and the result goes out the next morning — every one, hit or miss, whether it makes the model look good or not.

By December there will be a real record on this page, and it will be worth something precisely because it was published before anyone knew how it would turn out.
